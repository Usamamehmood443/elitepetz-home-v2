Evolution of Governance Policies

ElitePetz governance policies are reviewed and revised over time. This page explains what can prompt a change, how a revision is approved and published, how each version is identified, and which version governs a certification determination.

The change process at a glance

Policies change through a recorded process.

A revision can

Change what a policy says

  • ✓Clarify wording without changing its effect.
  • ✓Add, revise, or remove a requirement.
  • ✓Restructure or consolidate documents.
  • ✓Supersede a version with a new one.
  • ✓Withdraw a document that no longer applies.
A revision cannot

Rewrite what has already happened

  • —Apply a new rule to a determination already made.
  • —Edit a published version without a new version number.
  • —Remove earlier versions from the retained record.
  • —Change a certification status on its own.
  • —Reuse a document ID for a different subject.

Practical effect: a reader can always identify which version of a policy was in force on a given date, and a certified entity is not judged against a rule that did not exist when the matter was reviewed.

01Why policies change

Governance documents are written to be applied. When applying them reveals a gap, an ambiguity, or a change in the environment they operate in, the document is revised rather than worked around.

Trigger

Operational experience

A rule proves unclear, produces inconsistent outcomes, or does not address a situation that has arisen in review or enforcement.

Trigger

Regulatory developments

A change in applicable law or regulation affects what the Authority may do, must record, or must disclose.

Trigger

Industry standards

Recognized practice in companion animal breeding, welfare, or certification moves in a way the published framework should reflect.

Trigger

Internal policy review

Every governance document is reviewed at least annually. A scheduled review may confirm the document unchanged or begin a revision.

A correction of a factual or typographical error is also a change and is recorded as one. It is not applied silently to the published text.

02How a change is made

Every revision passes through the same stages. A change that has not completed them is not in force.

Step 01

Proposal

The need for a change is documented: what prompted it, which document and sections are affected, and the intended effect.

Step 02

Drafting and review

The Governance function prepares the revised text and reviews it against the rest of the framework so that related policies stay consistent. Conflict-of-interest safeguards apply to everyone involved.

Step 03

Approval

The revision is approved by the function authorized to do so under the Decision-Making Authority policy. Approval is recorded with the date and the approving function.

Step 04

Publication and effective date

The approved version is published with its version number and effective date. Material amendments are communicated to active certified entities before they take effect unless an earlier effective date is required by law or by an urgent animal-welfare or certification-integrity risk.

Step 05

Record

The version history on the document is updated, the previous version is marked Superseded, and the change is retained as part of the governance record.

03Versions, status, and effective dates

Each governance document carries the same four identifiers at the top of its page. Together they tell a reader exactly which text they are looking at and whether it is in force.

IdentifierWhat it tells you
Document IDA permanent reference for the document, such as EP-GOV-EGP-001. It identifies the subject, not the version, so it does not change when the document is revised and is not reused for another subject.
VersionWhich revision of the document this is. A clarification that does not change the effect of the document is a minor revision (for example, 1.0 to 1.1). A change to a requirement, deadline, or scope is a new major version (for example, 1.x to 2.0).
Effective dateThe date from which this version governs. It may be later than the publication date so that affected parties have notice.
StatusWhether this version is in force. The statuses used across all governance documents are defined below.
StatusMeaning
DraftUnder preparation or review. Not approved and not in force. Not published on the public site.
Approved, not yet effectiveApproved and published, with an effective date in the future. The previous version remains in force until that date.
CurrentIn force. This is the version applied to any matter reviewed on or after its effective date.
SupersededReplaced by a later version. Retained in the record and still relevant to matters reviewed while it was current.
WithdrawnNo longer applies and has not been replaced. Retained in the record.

04Which version applies

Controlling rule

Certification status determinations remain subject to the policies in effect at the time of review.

A determination is made under the version of each relevant policy whose status was Current on the date the matter was reviewed. A later revision does not reopen a determination that has already been made, and an earlier version is not applied to a matter reviewed after it was superseded. This is subject to controlling law and the applicable certification agreement.

Written decisions identify the document ID and version applied. Where a matter is under review when a new version takes effect, the notice of review states which version governs.

Superseded versions remain part of the retained governance record so that a decision can be read against the text that was in force when it was made.

05Where changes are published

There is no separate change log. Each governance document records its own history in its final section, in the same format on every page.

ColumnWhat it records
VersionThe version number of that revision.
PublishedThe date the revision was published on this site.
EffectiveThe date from which the revision governs.
SummaryWhat changed. For a minor revision, the summary states that the effect is unchanged.
StatusThe current status of that revision: Current, Superseded, or Withdrawn.

The Governance Framework page maps every governance document and links to the current version of each. A change to that map is itself recorded on the Governance Framework page.
